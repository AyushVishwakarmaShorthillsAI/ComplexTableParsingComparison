### 4. Table Data
This section of the table details three patients with homozygous variants in the *MMP15* gene.

**Patient 14** has a homozygous frameshift variant in the *MMP15* gene, identified as NM_002428.3:c.1058delC, which results in the protein change p.Pro353fs. This patient comes from a family with a history of consanguinity. The patient's phenotype is complex and multisystemic, including soft palate, constipation, developmental regression, dysphagia, dyspnea, failure to thrive, feeding difficulties, fetal distress, gastroesophageal reflux, global developmental delay, and intestinal malrotation. Physical features include joint hypermobility, microretrognathia, muscular hypotonia, a pointed chin, hypertelorism, a wide nasal bridge, and a webbed neck. The patient also presented with jaundice, cyanosis, and cholestasis with elevated hepatic transaminases. Severe congenital heart defects were observed, including a ventricular septal defect, atrial septal defect, double outlet right ventricle, hypoplastic left heart, and tricuspid regurgitation. The variant was observed to cosegregate in an affected sibling.

**Patient 15** is the affected sibling of Patient 14 and carries the same homozygous frameshift variant in the *MMP15* gene (NM_002428.3:c.1058delC, p.Pro353fs). The phenotype for this patient includes an abnormal facial shape, antenatal onset of issues, conjugated hyperbilirubinemia, decreased body weight, deeply set eyes, failure to thrive, hypertelorism, and intrauterine growth retardation. The patient also had jaundice, left atrial enlargement, left ventricular hypertrophy, low-set ears, a patent foramen ovale, a pointed chin, a prominent forehead, pulmonary arterial hypertension, tachycardia, a triangular face, and a ventricular septal defect.

**Patient 16** has a homozygous missense variant in the *MMP15* gene, identified as NM_002428.3:c.691G>A, leading to the protein change p.Gly231arg. The patient's parents are consanguineous. The clinical phenotype includes nephrocalcinosis, cholestasis, intrauterine growth retardation, hepatomegaly, and elevated hepatic transaminases. Cardiac defects include both a ventricular septal defect and an atrial septal defect.

### 5. Key Findings

*   The clinical presentation includes significant congenital heart defects (such as ventricular and atrial septal defects), liver dysfunction (cholestasis, hepatomegaly, elevated transaminases), and developmental issues (intrauterine growth retardation, developmental delay).
*   Two different homozygous variants are described: a frameshift (p.Pro353fs) and a missense (p.Gly231arg), both leading to severe phenotypes.
*   The p.Pro353fs variant was found in two affected siblings, demonstrating cosegregation with the disease.
*   The proposed evidence suggests that *MMP15* may be involved in heart development through endothelial-to-mesenchymal transformation and endocardial cushion development, potentially via interaction with the transcription factor Snai1.
