### 4. Table Data
This table details findings for four patients with variants in the BLOC1S1 gene.

**Patients 1 and 2** are siblings from a consanguineous family. Both have the same homozygous variant in the BLOC1S1 gene, identified as NM_001487.3:c.284C>A, which results in a p.Ala95Asp protein change. Their shared phenotype includes abnormal myelination, generalized-onset seizures, intellectual disability, leukodystrophy, optic atrophy, seizures, severe global developmental delay, and spasticity. An unaffected sister is noted to be heterozygous for this variant.

**Patient 3**, from a family with no reported consanguinity, has a homozygous BLOC1S1 variant, NM_001487.3:c.185T>C, leading to a p.Leu62Pro protein change. This patient's clinical features include delayed myelination, cerebral hypomyelination, severe intellectual disability, severe global developmental delay, epilepsy, spastic tetraparesis, dystonia, severe visual impairment, and optic atrophy.

**Patient 4**, from a consanguineous family, has a homozygous BLOC1S1 variant, NM_001487.3:c.218+3A>G (also noted as g.56110792A>G). The effect on the protein is uncertain (p.?). The patient's phenotype includes arachnodactyly, congenital onset developmental regression, global developmental delay, intellectual disability, leukodystrophy, leukoencephalopathy, muscular hypotonia, and talipes equinovarus.

For all four patients, the supporting evidence links the findings to the function of BLOC1S1 as a component of the BLOC1 multisubunit protein complex. Loss-of-function variants in other genes of this complex, such as DTNBP1 and BLOC1S3, are known to cause Hermansky-Pudlak syndrome. This syndrome is characterized by pigmentation defects due to issues with lysosome-related organelles (pigment granules) and abnormal glutamatergic transmission. The associated literature is referenced by PMID 15102850 and OMIM entries 614076 and 614077.
