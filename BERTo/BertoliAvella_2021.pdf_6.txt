### 4. Table Data
This section of the table presents data for four patients with homozygous mutations in the *IPO8* gene.

**Patient 10** has a homozygous (Hom) splice site mutation in the *IPO8* gene, identified as NM_006390.3: c.1881 + 1G>A (genomic coordinate g.30814074C>T). The effect on the protein is unknown (p.?). The observed clinical phenotype includes pneumothorax, sparse scalp hair, tortuous cerebral arteries, right ventricular dilatation, and a depressed nasal bridge.

**Patient 11** presents with a homozygous (Hom) deletion in the *IPO8* gene, specifically NM_006390.3: c.2695 + 4_2695 + 8del (genomic coordinate g.30789908_30789912del). The protein-level effect is unknown (p.?). This patient is from a family with both consanguinity and a positive family history ("Yes, yes"). The extensive phenotype includes hydronephrosis, macroglossia (enlarged tongue), deeply set eyes, hyperextensible skin, muscular hypotonia, joint hypermobility, failure to thrive, ventricular septal defect, a high and narrow palate, genu valgum (knock-knees), short stature, prominent superficial blood vessels, external ear malformation, and a bifid tongue.

**Patient 12** carries a homozygous (Hom) nonsense mutation in the *IPO8* gene, NM_006390.3:c.776G>A, which results in a premature stop codon (p.Trp259*). This patient's family has a history of consanguinity and the condition ("Yes, yes"). The complex phenotype includes micrognathia (small jaw), posteriorly rotated ears, macrotia (large ears), abnormal sclera morphology, muscular hypotonia, global developmental delay, hip dysplasia, joint laxity, polyhydramnios, atrial septal defect, gastroesophageal reflux, intestinal malrotation, an abnormal branching pattern of the aortic arch, a right aortic arch with a retroesophageal left subclavian artery, epidural hemorrhage, and malposition of the stomach.

**Patient 13** has a homozygous (Hom) missense mutation in the *IPO8* gene, NM_006390.3:c.950A>G, leading to an amino acid change (p.Tyr317Cys). This patient is from a consanguineous family ("Yes"), but further family history is unknown. The clinical features include congenital onset failure to thrive, global developmental delay, mitral valve prolapse, muscular hypotonia, patent ductus arteriosus, patent foramen ovale, pulmonary arterial hypertension, ventricular septal defect, joint hypermobility, abnormal facial shape, an abnormal inflammatory response, atrial septal defect, bifid uvula, and a cleft.
