*   **Consistent Severe Phenotype:** Despite a wide variety of underlying genetic mutations (including insertions, deletions, and point mutations, both heterozygous and compound heterozygous), patients consistently present with a severe and overlapping set of clinical features.
*   **Core Symptoms:** The most frequently observed phenotypes across nearly all patients include growth failure, cachexia/bird-like faces, mental retardation (predominantly severe), microcephaly, cataracts, microphthalmia, retinal degeneration, deafness, and clinical photosensitivity.
*   **Early Onset:** The age of symptom onset is typically very early, often at birth or within the first year of life, indicating a congenital or early developmental disorder.
*   **Skin Photosensitivity:** A high prevalence of Fitzpatrick Skin Type V (indicating extreme photosensitivity) is noted across most patients.
*   **Genetic Heterogeneity:** The table demonstrates significant genetic heterogeneity, with numerous distinct mutations leading to similar clinical outcomes, highlighting the critical role of the affected gene in these pathways.
*   **Compound Heterozygosity:** Several patients exhibit compound heterozygous mutations, where two different mutations in the same gene contribute to the disease, resulting in a phenotype similar to those with single severe mutations.
