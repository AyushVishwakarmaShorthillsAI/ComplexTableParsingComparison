4.  **Table Data**

    The table presents data for five patients, all with variants in the *PLK1* gene.

    **Patients 17, 18, 19, and 20** share the same genetic variant: NM_005030.5:c.785G>A at the cDNA level, resulting in a p.Arg262Gln protein change. This variant is homozygous in all four patients. Patients 17, 18, and 19 have a positive family history of consanguinity, while Patient 20 does not.

    The phenotypes associated with this c.785G>A variant vary:
    *   **Patient 17** exhibits focal-onset seizures.
    *   **Patient 18** presents with a more extensive set of symptoms including an arachnoid cyst, cerebellar vermis hypoplasia, decreased body weight, delayed myelination, global developmental delay, microcephaly, oligohydramnios, posterior fossa cyst, premature birth, seizures, short chin, and short stature.
    *   **Patient 19** shows generalized tonic-clonic seizures, global developmental delay, and hyperpigmentation of the skin.
    *   **Patient 20** experiences global developmental delay, microcephaly, and intractable seizures.

    **Patient 21** has a different homozygous variant in the *PLK1* gene: NM_005030.5:c.408+3G>A (also noted as NC_000016.9:g.23690664G>A), with the protein change being unknown (p.?). This patient also has a history of consanguinity. The phenotype for Patient 21 is severe and includes abnormal myelination, brain atrophy, epileptic encephalopathy, generalized-onset seizure, hyperreflexia, infantile encephalopathy, intellectual disability, leukodystrophy, and leukoencephalopathy.

    For all five patients, the "Evidence" column consistently refers to the *WDR62* gene. It states that *WDR62* encodes a protein that localizes to the centrosome and nucleus depending on the cell cycle and type. *WDR62* is identified as a *PLK1* substrate, phosphorylated at Ser 897, which promotes astral microtubule assembly and spindle orientation. The evidence also notes that Primary Microcephaly Type 2 (MCPH2) is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous variants in the *WDR62* gene. All evidence points to the same PubMed ID: 28973348. The "Others" column is empty for all entries.
