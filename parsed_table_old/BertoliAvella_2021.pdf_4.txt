**4. Table Data:**

The table presents data for four patients, all identified with homozygous variants in the BLOC1S1 gene.

**Patient 1** exhibits a BLOC1S1 variant at the cDNA level, specifically NM_001487.3:c.284C>A, which results in a p.Ala95Asp protein change. This patient has a homozygous variant and a documented consanguineous family history. The observed phenotype includes abnormal myelination, generalized-onset seizure, intellectual disability, leukodystrophy, optic atrophy, seizures, severe global developmental delay, and spasticity. The evidence provided states that BLOC1S1 is a component of the ubiquitously expressed BLOC1 multisubunit protein complex. Loss-of-function variants in two genes (DTNBP1 and BLOC1S3) that form this complex are known to cause Hermansky-Pudlak syndrome. Furthermore, mutant mice lacking the conserved Blos1 subunit show eye pigmentation defects due to abnormal pigment granules (lysosome-related organelles) and abnormal glutamatergic transmission and behavior. The associated PMIDs are 15102850, OMIM 614076, and 614077. Notably, an unaffected sister of Patient 1 is heterozygous for this variant.

**Patient 2** also presents with a homozygous BLOC1S1 variant, identical to Patient 1: NM_001487.3:c.284C>A, leading to a p.Ala95Asp protein change. This patient also has a consanguineous family history, specifically noted as a "sibling above" (referring to Patient 1). The phenotype is consistent with Patient 1, including abnormal myelination, generalized-onset seizure, intellectual disability, leukodystrophy, optic atrophy, seizures, severe global developmental delay, and spasticity. The evidence and PMIDs are indicated as "As above," meaning they are the same as for Patient 1.

**Patient 3** has a different homozygous BLOC1S1 variant: NM_001487.3:c.185T>C, resulting in a p.Leu62Pro protein change. Unlike the first two patients, there is no reported consanguineous family history for Patient 3. The phenotype for this patient includes delayed myelination, cerebral hypomyelination, severe intellectual disability, severe global developmental delay, epilepsy, spastic tetraparesis, dystonia, severe visual impairment, and optic atrophy. The evidence and PMIDs are also "As above," referring to the information provided for Patient 1.

**Patient 4** shows a homozygous BLOC1S1 variant, NM_001487.3:c.218+3A>G, which also includes the genomic coordinate NC_000012.11:g.56110792A>G. The resulting protein change is unknown (p.?). This patient has a consanguineous family history. The phenotype is distinct and includes arachnodactyly, congenital onset, developmental regression, global developmental delay, intellectual disability, leukodystrophy, leukoencephalopathy, muscular hypotonia, and talipes equinovarus. The evidence and PMIDs are again "As above," referencing the details from Patient 1.

*   Patients 1 and 2 share the same BLOC1S1 variant (c.284C>A, p.Ala95Asp) and exhibit a highly similar neurological phenotype characterized by myelination abnormalities, seizures, intellectual disability, and global developmental delay.
