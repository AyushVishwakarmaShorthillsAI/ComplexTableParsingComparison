**2. Table Purpose**
This table presents a detailed cephalometric analysis for ten individual patients (Patient 8 through Patient 17), comparing their specific measurements in both norma lateralis and norma frontalis views against age-corresponding cephalometric standards. The purpose is to highlight individual patient variations (increased, reduced, or normal) relative to these standards, providing a comprehensive overview of their craniofacial morphology.

**3. Table Structure**
The table is structured with "Measurement" as the primary row header, listing various cephalometric parameters grouped under "Norma lateralis" (further subdivided into "Typology," "Skeletal analysis," and "Denture analysis") and "Norma frontalis." The columns represent individual patients (Patient 8, Patient 9, Patient 10, Patient 11, Patient 12, Patient 14, Patient 15, Patient 16, Patient 17), each identified by their patient number and age in years and months (e.g., (6.7) for Patient 8). *   **Consistent Skeletal and Denture Protrusion:** All patients consistently show "Increased" values for skeletal analysis parameters (Convexity and ANB) and specific denture analysis parameters (i to APg in both mm and degrees), indicating a general tendency towards a more protrusive skeletal and dental profile compared to age-corresponding standards.
*   **Widespread Reduction in Typology and Frontal Measurements:** A vast majority of typology measurements (Facial depth, Lower facial height, Mandibular arc, FMA, AFH, PFH, FHI) and all norma frontalis measurements (Cranial width, Bifrontotemporale width, Bizygomatic width, Nasal width, Bigonial width) are indicated as "Reduced" across all patients. This suggests a general reduction in overall facial dimensions and specific facial height and width parameters.
*   **Discrepancy in Facial Axis Interpretation:** There is a notable inconsistency in the "CS" column for "Facial axis." While all patients are marked as "Increased" (↑), many of their actual measurements (e.g., Patient 8: 81.0 vs. standard 89.5) are numerically lower than the standard mean, contradicting the "Increased" label. This suggests a potential error in the labeling or interpretation of this specific parameter's clinical significance.
*   **Individual Normal Variations:** Despite the overarching trends, some patients exhibit normal values for specific parameters, such as Patient 16 for Lower facial height and IMPA, and Patient 8 for IMPA.
*   **Missing Data:** Data for FMIA is missing for Patients 11 and 12.
