### 5. Key Findings
*   The table highlights a consistent core set of clinical manifestations in homozygous CS-B patients, including growth failure, low birth weight, cachexia/bird-like faces, mental retardation, microcephaly, cataracts, clinical photosensitivity, and dental anomalies, regardless of the specific *ERCC8* mutation or clinical classification (I, II, or III).
*   Conversely, retinal degeneration and arthrogryposis were notably absent in all 27 homozygous patients presented, suggesting these may not be universal features of CS-B or are less common in this specific genetic subgroup.
*   The age of symptom onset for CS-B is predominantly very early, with many patients experiencing onset at birth (0 years) or within the first few years of life.
*   A wide spectrum of *ERCC8* gene mutations, including deletions, duplications, point mutations, and insertions, leading to various protein alterations (e.g., frameshifts, premature stop codons, amino acid substitutions, or deletions), are associated with CS-B.
*   Despite the genetic heterogeneity and varying clinical classifications, the shared phenotypic profile underscores the fundamental impact of *ERCC8* dysfunction in CS-B.
